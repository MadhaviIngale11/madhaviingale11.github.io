About

My route into product was not a straight line. I studied computer science, moved into business analytics for my MBA, and spent the time in between doing the unglamorous work — cleaning data, validating vendor APIs, chasing down why a transaction failed at 2am.

That mix turned out to be useful. In fintech I learned how much of a product lives in its edge cases. At Neuramach.ai I work closer to the front of the process: sitting with founders, pulling apart a vague business problem, and writing it down as something an engineering team can actually pick up.

The part I like most is the ambiguous middle — when nobody has agreed what the problem is yet. I am comfortable asking uncomfortable questions there, then turning the answers into requirements, user stories and acceptance criteria that hold up under scrutiny.

I read and write enough SQL, Python and API documentation to have a real conversation with engineers. I do not want to be the one shipping the code. I want to be the one making sure the right thing gets shipped.

Case studies

Three problems, and how I worked through them.

AI-powered personalized skincare recommendation system

Python · Scikit-learn · NLP

Problem
Shoppers face thousands of skincare products and an unreadable volume of reviews. Choosing the right one is guesswork, and the wrong choice has a real cost for the user.
Approach
Built a recommendation system using SVM classification over a dataset of more than 8,000 products and over 1 million reviews.
Data
Product attributes paired with a large review corpus, cleaned and structured before modelling.
AI / ML
Applied NLP with TF-IDF, BERT embeddings and sentiment extraction to improve the quality of what the system recommended.
Product opportunity
A recommendation layer like this fits naturally into a retailer's product page or onboarding quiz. The product question is not accuracy alone — it is whether the user trusts the suggestion enough to buy, which points to explaining why something was recommended.
What I learned
Model quality and product quality are not the same thing. A technically better recommendation still fails if the user cannot see the reasoning behind it.

ESG dashboard for technology companies

Power BI · Excel

Problem
ESG performance for large technology companies sits scattered across reports and formats, which makes comparison slow and inconsistent.
Approach
Built an interactive dashboard to visualise ESG trends and KPIs for leading technology companies, with slicers and drill-through so a viewer can move from an overview into a single company without losing context.
What it does
KPI tracking, interactive filtering, drill-through detail, and a business intelligence layer built for decision support rather than reporting alone.
Product thinking
A dashboard is a product with users. The question is not "what data do we have" but "what decision is this person about to make". Filters and drill-through exist so an analyst can follow a hunch — spot a trend at the group level, then check whether one company is driving it. If a dashboard cannot answer the next question a user will ask, it becomes a screenshot in a deck.
What I learned
Deciding what to leave out was harder, and mattered more, than deciding what to include.

Taxi ride data analysis

Python · Pandas · Matplotlib

Problem
Does the payment method a rider chooses relate to the revenue of the ride? A useful question for anyone deciding how to nudge payment behaviour.
Approach
Ran exploratory data analysis and hypothesis testing to evaluate revenue differences between payment methods.
Method
EDA, statistical analysis using a T-test, and data visualisation to make the comparison legible.
Product opportunity
A finding like this only becomes a product decision once you know whether the relationship is causal. Correlation here would justify an experiment, not a rollout.
What I learned
How to state a hypothesis before touching the data, and how to hold back from over-reading a result that a statistical test does not support.
